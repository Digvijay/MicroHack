This document is fictional and is used for training only.

## 1. Purpose

1.1 Strandvik Drives AB keeps a small stock of SD-700 and SD-900 drive units in the Rotterdam warehouse and at the Pune factory. Customers plan their maintenance stops around these units. This policy makes sure that a unit promised to one customer is not quietly given to another.

## 2. Scope

2.1 This policy applies to every reservation in the ERP system (format RSV-NNNN), to every Strandvik employee and to every Strandvik agent that reads or proposes changes to reservations.

## 3. Definitions

3.1 **Reservation**: a record that ties one drive unit, by serial, to one customer, machine and date.

3.2 **Owner**: the key account manager (KAM) named on the reservation. The owner is the only person who can release it.

3.3 **Release**: a clear statement from the owner that the unit is no longer needed for the reservation.

3.4 **Reallocation**: moving a reserved unit to a different customer.

## 4. Rules

4.1 A reserved unit belongs to its reservation until the **owner** releases it.

4.2 A note, an email or a forecast is **not** a release. **A note is a reason to ask.** If anything suggests that a reservation may no longer be needed, ask the owner. Do not assume.

4.3 Reallocation needs the owner's approval in writing (a chat message counts).

4.4 The reserved customer must still get an equal unit in time. An equal unit is the same model, in new or equal condition, delivered before the reservation's needed-by date.

4.5 The KAM who reallocates is accountable for the cost risk, for example extra freight to get the equal unit to the reserved customer in time.

4.6 When a unit is reallocated, the original reservation is moved to the equal unit in the ERP system the same day, and the reallocation is recorded on both customers' cases.

## 5. What agents may do

5.1 Agents may read reservations, find the owner and ask the owner.

5.2 Agents may prepare a reallocation proposal. They may not release, move or reallocate a reservation themselves.

## 6. Example

Rotterdam holds an SD-900 reserved for a customer's maintenance stop. A colleague wrote last week that the stop "will probably move". That note is not a release. The right action is to ask the owner of the reservation, get the answer in writing, and make sure the reserved customer still gets an equal unit in time.
