Microsoft IQ has two kinds of graph, and teams always ask which one to use. On the left, Foundry IQ can extract a knowledge graph from your documents, guided by an ontology you write. On the right, Fabric IQ has an ontology too, but it's bound to your governed tables. Both are in preview.

Same question to both: which customers depend on Nordhavn? The first graph we built missed customers. The extractor created two nodes for one product: "PV-400" and "PV-400 pump valve". Brennmark and Østerdal hung off the second one, so walking from HX-220 only found Solvik. That's entity resolution, and it's the classic graph problem.

[click] The fix was a few words of naming guidance in the ontology: name a product by its code only. (I let the node merge down.) Now the walk finds exactly three: Brennmark, Østerdal and Solvik. And not Ferrovia, who buys a ball valve without the housing. On the right, Fabric IQ gets the same three by joining five tables, and it can add the money: 1,975,000 euros.

What went wrong with all the context, and the rule that fixed each failure.

Observed

Playbook rule

For a delivery decision, always do all four steps: Work, Fabric, Foundry, Web.

Observed

Playbook rule

Emails and chats are newer than the lakehouse. Prefer the newest dated source.

Playbook rule

A customer's “we could accept” is a ceiling, not our offer.
